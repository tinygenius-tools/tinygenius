How to Calculate Your Freelance Hourly Rate in 2026

Setting your freelance hourly rate can be challenging. Charge too little, and you may struggle to cover your expenses. The right rate should reflect your income goals, business costs and the actual hours you can bill clients.

Here's a simple method to calculate your required hourly rate.

1. Start With Your Annual Income Goal

First, decide how much you want to earn annually from your freelance business.

For example, suppose your desired annual income is $60,000.

2. Calculate Your Annual Business Expenses

Include costs such as:

  • Software subscriptions
  • Website hosting and domain expenses
  • Equipment and maintenance
  • Marketing and client acquisition
  • Internet and communication
  • Professional services

Suppose your annual business expenses total $6,000.

Annual revenue requirement:

$60,000 + $6,000 = $66,000

3. Calculate Your Actual Billable Hours

Not every hour you work is an hour you can charge a client for. Freelancers also spend time on proposals, administration, meetings, marketing and finding new clients.

Let's assume:

  • 25 billable hours per week
  • 48 working weeks per year
Annual billable hours:

25 × 48 = 1,200 hours

4. Calculate Your Required Hourly Rate

Use this formula:

Hourly Rate =

(Annual Income Goal + Annual Business Expenses) ÷ Annual Billable Hours

Using our example:

$66,000 ÷ 1,200 =

$55 / HOUR

Your estimated required hourly rate is $55. This is a planning estimate, not a guaranteed market rate.

5. Consider Your Experience and Market

Your calculated rate is a starting point, not necessarily the final price you should quote. Consider your experience, specialist skills, project complexity, client budgets and market demand.

Also account separately for taxes, benefits, savings and other financial obligations where applicable. The simple formula above does not calculate these automatically.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should a beginner freelancer charge per hour?

There is no universal beginner rate. It depends on your service, skills, experience, location and the value you provide.

How many billable hours should a freelancer work per week?

It varies by profession and business model. A freelancer working 40 hours may have fewer billable hours after accounting for administration, marketing and client communication.

Should freelancers include business expenses in their rates?

Yes. Business expenses affect how much revenue your business needs to generate to meet its financial goals.

Is hourly pricing better than project pricing?

Neither is universally better. Hourly pricing can work well when project scope is uncertain, while fixed project pricing can suit clearly defined deliverables.

Final Thoughts

A sustainable freelance business starts with understanding your numbers. Don't choose your rate simply because another freelancer charges the same amount.

Calculate your income requirements, account for expenses and estimate the hours you can realistically bill.
