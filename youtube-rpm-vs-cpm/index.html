YouTube RPM vs CPM: What's the Difference and Which One Should Creators Use?

If you are estimating YouTube earnings, RPM and CPM are two metrics you will see in YouTube Analytics. They are related, but they measure different things. CPM describes advertiser spending on ad impressions. RPM is a creator-focused measure of revenue per 1,000 views.

The quick answer

RPM helps creators understand how much revenue they earn per 1,000 video views. CPM measures how much advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions before YouTube's revenue share. For a simple creator revenue estimate based on views, RPM is generally the more useful starting point.

1. What is YouTube RPM?

RPM means Revenue Per Mille (revenue per thousand). It represents the revenue a creator earns per 1,000 video views. YouTube describes RPM as including several revenue sources reported in Analytics, such as ads, YouTube Premium, channel memberships, Super Chat and Super Stickers. For standard videos it uses views; for Shorts it uses engaged views.

RPM formula
RPM = (Creator revenue ÷ Views) × 1,000

Example: if the revenue counted in the metric is $240 across 120,000 views, RPM is $2.00.

2. What is YouTube CPM?

CPM means Cost Per Mille. It measures the amount advertisers pay for 1,000 ad impressions. It is an advertiser-focused metric and is measured before YouTube's revenue share. An ad impression is counted when an ad is displayed; it is not the same as a video view.

CPM formula
CPM = (Advertiser ad spend ÷ Ad impressions) × 1,000

Example: if advertisers spend $600 for 200,000 ad impressions, CPM is $3.00.

4. What is playback-based CPM?

YouTube also reports playback-based CPM. It measures advertiser cost per 1,000 video playbacks in which an ad was shown. One video playback can include more than one ad impression, so playback-based CPM and CPM are not interchangeable. YouTube's official example shows how the two can differ when some playbacks include multiple ads.

For creator earnings, continue to use your actual RPM rather than converting CPM or playback-based CPM directly into revenue per view.

5. Why is RPM usually lower than CPM?

They use different denominators and represent different sides of the transaction. RPM is calculated after YouTube's revenue share and includes all views, including views where no ad was shown. CPM is based on ad impressions and advertiser spending before revenue share. Therefore, multiplying CPM by total video views will not give a reliable estimate of creator earnings.

Not every view includes an ad. Whether an ad appears can depend on advertiser demand, viewer geography, ad suitability, ad availability, viewer settings and other factors.

6. How to estimate YouTube revenue using RPM

For a simple planning estimate, use the RPM you observe in your own YouTube Analytics. Do not treat a generic RPM figure as a guaranteed rate: actual results vary by channel, content, audience, format, season and monetization mix.

Illustrative calculation

Monthly views: 100,000

Assumed RPM: $2.50

100,000 ÷ 1,000 × $2.50 = $250

Estimated monthly revenue: $250. This is an arithmetic example, not a typical or promised RPM.

The calculator uses your entered monthly views and RPM to produce an estimate. It does not access your YouTube account or predict what advertisers will pay.

7. What can make RPM change?

  • Audience geography: advertiser demand and competition vary across markets.
  • Content topic and audience: advertisers value audiences differently.
  • Seasonality: advertiser spending can change throughout the year.
  • Monetized-view share: a larger share of views without ads can lower RPM even if ad revenue is steady.
  • Revenue mix: memberships, Premium and fan-funding features can affect the RPM metric.
  • Video format: Shorts and standard videos use different view bases in RPM reporting.

8. When should creators use RPM or CPM?

Use RPM when you want to estimate creator revenue from views or compare the revenue efficiency of your channel over time. Use CPM when you want to understand advertiser demand and the value advertisers place on ad impressions. Review both in YouTube Analytics rather than treating them as interchangeable.

Frequently asked questions

Is RPM the same as earnings per 1,000 views?

Yes. RPM is the creator-focused revenue metric per 1,000 views (with Shorts using engaged views), and may include several YouTube revenue sources.

Does CPM mean the creator receives that amount?

No. CPM is advertiser spending per 1,000 ad impressions before YouTube's revenue share. It is not the creator's take-home revenue per 1,000 video views.

Can I calculate revenue using CPM and total views?

Not reliably. CPM is based on ad impressions, while total views include views without ads and may have different ad-impression counts. Use your channel's RPM for a basic view-based estimate.

Does RPM include sponsorships and merchandise?

YouTube's RPM metric does not include many revenue streams earned outside the YouTube Analytics revenue sources, such as most brand sponsorships, merchandise sales or services.

Where can I find RPM and CPM?

Open YouTube Studio, select Analytics, and review the Revenue tab. YouTube's help article explains the metrics and their definitions.
