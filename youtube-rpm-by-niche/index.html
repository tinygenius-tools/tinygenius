YouTube RPM by Niche for 2027–2028: What 2026 Benchmarks Can—and Can't—Tell You

YouTube RPM can vary sharply from one channel to another—even inside the same niche. This guide compares published 2026 benchmarks across 13 content categories, explains what those numbers can and cannot tell you, and shows how to estimate revenue using your own RPM.

Important: The niche figures below are historical sample benchmarks, not official YouTube rates and not a forecast for 2027–2028. RPM varies with audience geography, season, format, monetised views and advertiser demand. Use your own YouTube Analytics RPM for planning whenever available.
Important: These are reported medians from a third-party sample of monetized channels, not rates published or guaranteed by YouTube. Your own RPM may be higher or lower depending on audience, format, monetization and time of year.

Which YouTube niche has the highest RPM?

In this particular dataset, Education & Science had the highest reported median long-form RPM at $10.22. Transport followed at $5.69. The overall median was about $2.30, so the education figure should not be treated as a typical result for every education channel.

Do not choose a channel topic based on one RPM ranking alone. Audience demand, your ability to make useful videos consistently, viewer retention, advertiser suitability and the geography of your audience all matter. A high benchmark is not useful if the topic is a poor fit for your expertise or viewers.

Why does YouTube RPM differ by niche?

  • Advertiser demand: Some audiences attract more advertiser competition than others.
  • Viewer location: Ad markets differ by country, language and audience profile.
  • Video format: Standard long-form videos and Shorts use different monetization mechanics and view bases.
  • Monetized views: Not every view is accompanied by an ad.
  • Seasonality: Advertiser budgets and demand can shift during the year.
  • Revenue mix: YouTube RPM can include multiple YouTube revenue sources, not only watch-page ads.

How much can 100,000 views earn?

The arithmetic is straightforward when you know your own RPM:

Estimated revenue = (Views ÷ 1,000) × RPM

For 100,000 views, an assumed RPM of $2.30 gives an illustrative estimate of $230. At an assumed RPM of $5, the estimate would be $500. These are examples using selected RPM inputs—not promises, averages for your channel or forecasts.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good RPM on YouTube in 2026?

There is no universal good RPM. Compare your channel with its own past performance and use niche benchmarks only as broad context. Format, geography, content and monetization mix can make results very different.

Is YouTube RPM the same as CPM?

No. RPM is a creator-focused revenue metric per 1,000 views, while CPM describes advertiser cost per 1,000 ad impressions. They use different denominators and are not interchangeable. See our RPM vs CPM guide.

Does YouTube publish official RPM rates by niche?

No universal RPM rate card applies to every channel. Public niche tables are third-party samples or estimates. Your own YouTube Analytics is the relevant source for your channel's RPM.

Do Shorts have the same RPM as long-form videos?

No. Shorts and standard videos have different monetization mechanics and reporting bases. Avoid applying a long-form RPM benchmark directly to Shorts views.

Can I earn the same RPM as the highest-ranked niche?

Not necessarily. A niche median describes a sample, not a guaranteed outcome. Your audience, channel, video format and monetization eligibility all influence actual revenue.
