UGC Usage Rights Pricing in 2027–2028: Charge for the Licence, Not Just the Video

When a brand pays you to create a UGC video, the creation fee and the right to use that video are two different parts of the deal. A brand may want to publish your content organically, run it as a paid ad, use it across several countries, or keep using it for a year. Those requests do not represent the same licence.

Short answer

Price UGC usage rights by defining where the content can appear, how it can be used, how long the permission lasts, where geographically it applies, and whether the brand receives extra rights such as creator-handle advertising, exclusivity or raw footage. Quote the creation fee separately from the licence fee and put the agreed scope in writing.

1. Separate the creation fee from the usage licence

Your creation fee compensates you for producing the deliverable: planning, scripting, filming, editing and agreed revisions. A usage licence compensates you for allowing the brand to use that deliverable under specified conditions.

Keeping these items separate makes the offer easier to explain and prevents a short production fee from accidentally granting broad or indefinite commercial rights.

2. Identify what the brand is asking to do

3. Build the licence price from the actual scope

There is no universal percentage or industry-wide multiplier that reliably prices every UGC licence. A short organic repost and a long-running, multi-market paid campaign have different commercial value. Avoid presenting a generic percentage as a guaranteed market rate.

Instead, start with your creation fee and decide on a separate licence amount that reflects the requested scope. You can use this quote structure:

UGC project quote structure

Creation and editing fee

+ Usage licence fee (platforms, paid or organic, duration and territory)

+ Creator-handle advertising authorisation, if requested

+ Exclusivity fee, if requested

+ Raw footage or additional deliverables, if requested

Total project quote = the sum of the agreed components

This is a pricing framework, not a fixed rate card. Set each amount based on your costs, the value and reach of the requested use, the length of the licence, the opportunity you give up and the terms you are comfortable granting.

4. Example: turn a vague request into a defined licence

Imagine a brand asks to use one finished video in paid social advertising. Before giving a price, clarify the platform, licence start date, end date, countries, whether creator-handle advertising is included, whether the brand can edit the video, and whether raw files are requested.

Once those details are agreed, list the production fee and licence fee as separate line items. If the brand later wants another six months, a new territory or additional platforms, you can discuss an extension rather than treating the original permission as unlimited.

5. Protect yourself with clear written terms

Before accepting the brief, confirm:
  • Exact deliverables and file formats.
  • Platforms and brand accounts permitted to publish.
  • Organic use versus paid advertising.
  • Licence duration and start date.
  • Territory and any geographic restrictions.
  • Creator-handle authorisation and access expiry, if applicable.
  • Exclusivity category and duration, if any.
  • Raw footage, editing and derivative-work permissions.
  • Payment amount, due date and late-payment terms.
  • Renewal process and additional usage fees.

6. A simple message you can send to a brand

Thanks for sharing the brief. I can quote the content creation separately from usage rights so the scope is clear for both sides.

Could you confirm the platforms, whether the content will be used organically or in paid ads, the intended licence duration and territory, and whether creator-handle advertising, exclusivity or raw footage is required?

Once I have those details, I’ll send an itemised quote covering production and the requested usage licence.

7. Common UGC usage-rights pricing mistakes

  • Agreeing to “all usage” without a definition: specify platforms, duration, territory and advertising permissions.
  • Granting perpetual use by accident: state an end date and how renewals are priced.
  • Including whitelisting automatically: treat creator-handle advertising as a separate permission with clear access controls.
  • Giving away raw footage without considering edits: define the files and permitted derivative uses.
  • Accepting broad exclusivity without a limit: define the competing category, geography and time period.
  • Using someone else's rate as a universal benchmark: your costs, scope, experience and rights granted may differ.

Frequently asked questions

Should UGC creators charge separately for usage rights?

Creators can quote usage rights separately from content production. This makes the licence scope and commercial permission visible to the brand.

How much should I charge for 30 days of paid usage?

There is no single reliable amount for every creator or campaign. Consider your creation fee, ad platforms, expected use, territory, content performance context if known, and the value of the permission being granted.

Are organic usage rights and paid usage rights the same?

No. Organic use generally means publishing through a brand's own channels without paid distribution. Paid usage means the brand can put advertising spend behind the content. Specify which permission is included.

What is whitelisting or creator-handle advertising?

It generally refers to a brand running advertising through or under a creator's social identity or account authorisation. Define the platform, access method, duration, campaign scope and revocation process before agreeing.

Should raw footage cost extra?

It can be a separate line item because unedited files may let a brand create additional edits or versions. Define which files are delivered and what the brand may do with them.

Can a brand use my UGC forever after paying once?

Not unless your agreement grants that permission. State whether the licence is time-limited or perpetual, and define the allowed platforms, territory and use.
