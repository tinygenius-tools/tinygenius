How Much Does YouTube Pay for 1,000, 10,000, 100,000 or 1 Million Views?

How much YouTube pays depends on more than view count. A useful first estimate uses your channel's RPM (revenue per 1,000 views). RPM varies by audience, topic, format, season, monetized views and revenue sources, so there is no single payout that applies to every channel.

Quick answer: multiply views by your RPM, then divide by 1,000. For example, at an assumed RPM of $2, 100,000 views produce an illustrative estimate of $200. This is a calculation example, not a typical rate or earnings promise.

1. YouTube earnings formula

Estimated revenue = (Views ÷ 1,000) × RPM

Use the RPM shown in your own YouTube Studio Analytics for a more relevant estimate. If you do not yet have channel data, test several clearly labelled assumptions instead of treating a public benchmark as guaranteed.

3. Why earnings differ between channels

  • Viewer location: advertiser demand and competition vary by country and audience.
  • Topic and audience: different audiences attract different advertiser demand.
  • Ad availability: not every video view includes an ad impression.
  • Seasonality: advertiser budgets and demand change over time.
  • Video format: Shorts and standard videos have different monetization mechanics and RPM view bases.
  • Revenue mix: YouTube RPM can include eligible revenue sources beyond advertising, such as Premium and fan-funding features.

4. Does one million views always mean thousands of dollars?

No. One million views is not a fixed payout. At the same view count, two channels can report different revenue because their RPM, audience mix, content, monetization eligibility and format differ. Use your own Analytics rather than assuming a viral view count guarantees a particular income.

5. Shorts vs long-form video estimates

Do not automatically apply a long-form RPM to Shorts views. YouTube Analytics uses engaged views for Shorts RPM, while standard video RPM uses views. Compare like with like and use the RPM metric for the format you are estimating.

6. Estimate your own YouTube revenue

Follow these steps:

  1. Open YouTube Studio and go to Analytics → Revenue.
  2. Choose a period that makes sense for your channel.
  3. Find your RPM for the relevant content format.
  4. Enter your expected views and RPM into the calculator.
  5. Treat the result as an estimate; actual revenue can change.

Frequently asked questions

How much does YouTube pay for 1,000 views?

There is no universal payout. Calculate it using your channel's RPM: RPM is revenue per 1,000 views (for Shorts, per 1,000 engaged views).

How much is 100,000 views worth?

Multiply 100 by your RPM. At an illustrative $2 RPM, the estimate is $200. Your actual result may be higher or lower.

How much does YouTube pay for 1 million views?

Multiply 1,000 by your RPM. At an illustrative $2 RPM, the estimate is $2,000; this is not a guaranteed or typical amount.

Can I use CPM to calculate my earnings?

Not directly. CPM measures advertiser cost per 1,000 ad impressions before YouTube's revenue share. RPM is the more useful creator metric for a view-based estimate. Read our YouTube RPM vs CPM guide.

Where do I find my RPM?

Open YouTube Studio, select Analytics and then the Revenue tab. YouTube explains these metrics in its official revenue analytics help article.
