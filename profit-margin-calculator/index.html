How the profit margin calculator works

The calculator separates the amount left after each sale from the fixed costs your business must cover over a month. That distinction helps you avoid treating sales revenue as profit.

Contribution per unit = Selling price − percentage fees − direct costs − other variable costs

Contribution margin is contribution per unit divided by selling price after percentage fees. Gross margin before fees is selling price minus the direct and other variable costs, divided by selling price. Markup compares the profit before percentage fees with those variable costs.

Margin and markup are not the same

Margin measures profit as a share of the selling price. Markup measures the amount added relative to cost. For example, a $60 profit on a $100 selling price is a 60% margin before fees. If the cost was $40, that same $60 profit is a 150% markup on cost.

Why payment fees and fixed overhead matter

A payment or marketplace fee reduces the revenue you retain from each sale. Fixed overhead is different: it is generally incurred whether you make one sale or many. The break-even estimate divides monthly fixed overhead by contribution per unit. It does not include a desired owner salary unless you add it to fixed overhead.

Use the target price as a pricing test

The target price estimate includes your entered variable costs, percentage fee and an equal allocation of monthly fixed overhead across your expected monthly sales volume. If sales volume changes, the overhead allocation and target price change too. Treat it as a planning scenario—not a guarantee that customers will accept the price.

Before you agree to a lower price

A discount reduces the amount available to cover costs and overhead. If a client or buyer asks for a lower price, consider trading the reduction for a smaller scope, fewer revisions, a shorter licence, a longer timeline or more favourable payment terms rather than giving away value without a change in return.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good profit margin?

There is no single margin that is right for every industry, product or service. Compare like-for-like businesses and account for your costs, risk, sales channel and customer value. This calculator does not provide industry benchmarks.

Does this calculator include tax?

No. It does not estimate income tax, sales tax, VAT or other statutory obligations. Add relevant costs to your own financial planning separately.

What if my contribution per unit is zero or negative?

Your current price does not cover the variable costs and percentage fees entered. The calculator will not show a finite break-even sales quantity in that situation.

Is this suitable for freelancers and creators?

Yes. You can treat a project as one unit, include your project labour and production expenses as direct costs, and include payment or platform fees. Add monthly business overhead to understand the sales volume needed to cover it.
