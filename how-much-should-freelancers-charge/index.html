How Much Should I Charge as a Freelancer? A Practical Pricing Guide

There is no single hourly rate that works for every freelancer. A sustainable quote should cover the time required, business costs and the amount of work you can realistically sell. It should also reflect the deliverables, complexity, deadlines, revisions and rights included in the agreement.

Start here: calculate the minimum rate your business needs, estimate the real effort for the project, then price the scope and terms—not just the number of hours you expect to spend.

1. Calculate your minimum sustainable hourly rate

Begin with your desired annual business income, annual operating expenses and realistic annual billable hours.

Minimum hourly rate = (Income goal + Business expenses) ÷ Annual billable hours

Example: a $60,000 income goal plus $6,000 in annual business expenses, divided by 1,200 billable hours, gives a planning rate of $55 per billable hour. This example is arithmetic, not a market benchmark. Account separately for taxes, benefits, savings and other obligations that apply to you.

3. Turn your hourly rate into a project quote

Estimate all the work needed to complete the agreed deliverables—not only the visible production time. Include discovery, research, planning, communication, production, quality checks, revisions and handover.

Illustrative project calculation

Estimated effort: 12 hours

Planning hourly rate: $55

12 × $55 = $660

$660 is the time-based starting estimate before any separately agreed costs, specialist expenses, licensing, rush work or scope changes. It is not a recommended market price.

4. Define the scope before you quote

  • Deliverables, quantity, format and specifications.
  • What the client must provide and when.
  • Milestones, review dates and final deadline.
  • Number of revision rounds and what counts as a revision.
  • What is excluded and how additional work is approved.
  • Usage rights, ownership, licensing or transfer terms.
  • Payment schedule, deposit, due dates and late-payment process.

5. Price complexity, urgency and additional rights transparently

A project with the same number of deliverables can require very different effort. Specialist research, technical complexity, short deadlines, additional stakeholders, extensive revisions or broader usage rights may change the quote. Identify these elements as separate line items or explain how they affect the project fee. Do not add arbitrary percentages without a reason you can explain.

6. Avoid common freelance pricing mistakes

For creators who combine freelance production with influencer or UGC deliverables, the quote may also need to account for audience distribution, licensing and exclusivity. See our creator brand deal pricing formula for a structured way to separate those components.

  • Using every working hour as billable time: proposals, marketing and administration also take time.
  • Quoting before clarifying the brief: unclear scope creates unpaid work and disagreement.
  • Including unlimited revisions: define the included rounds and change-request process.
  • Ignoring expenses: software, equipment, payment fees and professional services affect your required revenue.
  • Copying another freelancer's rate: their costs, experience, location, scope and client base may differ.
  • Discounting without changing scope: if a client has a lower budget, consider reducing deliverables or changing the timeline rather than silently lowering the value of the same work.

7. What to say when a client asks for your rate

Thanks for sharing the project details. Before I confirm a fee, I'd like to align on the deliverables, timeline, revision rounds and intended usage. I'll then send an itemised proposal with the project fee, what's included and the process for any additional work.

Frequently asked questions

Should freelancers charge hourly or per project?

Use hourly pricing when effort or scope is uncertain and fixed project pricing when deliverables and acceptance criteria are clear. Either model can work when terms are explicit.

How do I know if my freelance rate is too low?

Compare your actual revenue and billable hours with your business costs and income goal. If the rate does not support the required revenue, revisit your pricing, costs, capacity or service mix.

Should I charge separately for revisions?

State how many revision rounds are included. Work beyond the agreed scope can be quoted separately, subject to client approval.

What if a client cannot afford my quote?

Discuss a smaller deliverable, phased work, a longer timeline or a different payment schedule. Make sure any lower fee corresponds to a clearly adjusted scope or terms.

How often should I review my rates?

Review them when costs, demand, expertise, capacity or the type of work you deliver materially changes. There is no universal review interval.
