How to calculate your freelance hourly rate

A freelance rate calculator helps independent professionals estimate how much they should charge clients for their work. It considers your desired income, business expenses and the number of hours you can realistically bill.

Whether you are a graphic designer, writer, developer, consultant or digital marketer, knowing your minimum hourly rate can help you price your services more confidently.

How is your freelance hourly rate calculated?

The calculator uses a simple formula:

Hourly rate = (Desired annual income + Annual expenses) ÷ Annual billable hours

Annual billable hours are calculated by multiplying your billable hours per week by your working weeks per year. For example, 25 billable hours per week across 48 working weeks gives 1,200 billable hours per year.

Worked example using the calculator's starting values

If your desired annual income is $60,000, annual business expenses are $6,000, and you can bill 25 hours per week for 48 weeks, your annual revenue target is $66,000 and your billable hours are 1,200.

$66,000 ÷ 1,200 = $55 per billable hour. This is an illustrative calculation using the form's default inputs, not a recommended market price.

What this hourly rate estimate includes—and what it does not

The calculation includes the annual income target and annual business expenses you enter. It divides that total by your estimated annual billable hours. It does not check competitor prices, demand for your services, or whether a particular client will accept the rate.

  • Taxes and statutory contributions: include the amount you need to cover in your income target or expenses where appropriate; the calculator does not estimate tax.
  • Unpaid work: marketing, administration, proposals, learning and client communication reduce the hours you can bill. Account for them by entering realistic billable hours.
  • Payment risk and fees: payment processing, currency conversion, late payment and bad-debt risk are not modelled separately.
  • Market positioning: experience, portfolio, specialisation, urgency and client value can justify a quote above this minimum.

Why should freelancers calculate their rates?

  • Set prices that account for business expenses.
  • Plan income goals more realistically.
  • Avoid undercharging for professional services.
  • Understand how available working hours affect pricing.
  • Build a more sustainable freelance business.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I charge as a freelancer?

Your rate depends on your desired income, business expenses, experience, skills and available billable hours. Use this calculator to estimate your minimum starting rate.

What are billable hours?

Billable hours are the hours you can directly charge clients for. Administrative work, marketing and business development may take additional time.

Should I include business expenses in my rate?

Yes. Software, equipment, internet, professional services and other business costs should be considered when setting your freelance prices.

Does this estimate include taxes?

No. The calculator adds the income target and business expenses you enter, then divides by annual billable hours. It does not calculate income tax, sales tax or social contributions. Plan for applicable taxes separately and seek local tax guidance where needed.

Is this the rate I should quote every client?

Not necessarily. It is a starting minimum based on your inputs, not a market-rate benchmark. Consider your experience, project complexity, client value, deadline, risk and the scope of work before preparing a quote.

How do I turn an hourly rate into a project price?

Estimate the total delivery and project-management hours, multiply by your chosen hourly rate, then account for revisions, expenses, usage rights, urgency and other agreed terms. Confirm the scope and payment schedule in writing.

Does this calculator convert currencies?

No. The currency selector changes the currency format only. Enter your income and expenses in the currency you select.
